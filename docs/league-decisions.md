# Root League: Decisions

[Document index](README.md) · [Player guide](player-guide.md)

Internal source of truth: what has been chosen, what was rejected and why, what is parked for later, and what is still open. Related: [implementation](league-implementation.md) (how it runs) and [variables](league-variables.md) (the knobs).

## Decided

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Score is a win. Nothing is awarded to other places | Final game score is not a good measure of how close a game was |
| 2 | Tables are 4 players, fixed for now | 4-player Root is the priority. 3-player Root changes balance and kingmaking |
| 3 | 8 players, 4 games each (8 games total) | Fits the group size and keeps the time commitment reasonable |
| 4 | Two tables run at once | The host can run two, at the cost of splitting resources |
| 5 | Rounds 1-3 are qualifying, round 4 is a final table plus a consolation table | Lets the last round be set by results, so exact pairing evenness matters less. Everyone still plays 4 games |
| 6 | The final table is the top 4 by wins. The champion is the final winner | Simple, and the title is decided by one game among the best records |
| 7 | Strength of schedule is the first tiebreaker for the cut | Pairings will not be perfectly even, and this corrects for who each player faced. Easier to fix than forcing exact pairings |
| 8 | Perfectly even pairings are not required | Rejected as a goal, see below |
| 9 | No restriction on repeating factions | Players may play the same faction in multiple games; neither a never-repeat rule nor a two-play cap is part of this league |

## Proposed, awaiting confirmation

- Final-table players pick seat and faction in standings order, so qualifying has stakes even when records are tied. Note: in AdSet the last seat picks first, so the best qualifier would take the last seat, which is also last in turn order.
- Rounds 1-3 are generated by script: minimize uneven meetings, avoid any pair meeting three times, balance seats.
- Strength of schedule means the total wins of everyone you sat with in rounds 1-3.
- Faction setup: AdSet (Advanced Setup) for every game. This is the proposed method for the player-facing draft, not yet a confirmed rule.
- Faction pool: a curated pool of about half the factions (Root Conquest does the same), with a fixed map and deck.
- Two tables drafting at once: use two pools with no factions in common, or stagger the drafts, because both tables draw on the same faction setup cards and pieces.

## Considered and rejected

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Placement points (1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th get different points) | Game score and finishing position do not show how close a game was. Win-only is cleaner |
| Raw victory-point totals as the score | Games stop at 30 VP, factions score at different speeds, and dominance and coalition wins are not comparable |
| 7 players, 7 games (every pair meets exactly twice) | Games cannot overlap, and total table time felt like too much |
| 8 players, 7 rounds (every pair meets exactly 3 times) | 7 games each is far too many |
| 9 players at 3-player tables, 4 rounds (every pair meets once) | Tables must stay at 4 players |
| 16 players, 5 rounds (every pair meets once) | Needs 4 tables per round and 16 players |
| 13 players, 13 games (every pair meets once) | Games cannot overlap, and 13 games is a lot |
| Fixed 4-round schedule (best possible: 12 pairs once, 12 twice, 4 three times) | Replaced by the final table, which makes round 4 meaningful and removes the need to force exact evenness |
| Making every pair meet equally often as a hard requirement | Not achievable at 8 players and 4 games each with 4-player tables. Strength of schedule handles the imbalance instead |
| Faction-repeat restrictions (never repeat or a two-play cap) | The user chose no restriction on repeating factions. Earlier simulations explored these restrictions; neither is part of the current format |

## Parked ideas (not for iteration one)

**Cross-table bans.** With the two tables seated next to each other, a player at one table could call out a faction they do not want to face, and that faction would then be placed in the other table's draft pool. This repeats until both pools are settled. It could add interaction between the tables and shape who faces what without a fixed rule.

Why it is parked:
- It needs both tables to draft at the same time and be in close contact, which adds coordination.
- Open questions: who may ban and how many, how each pool stays legal and the same size, whether it conflicts with the pick order (last seat picks first in AdSet), and how faction availability works across the two tables.
- It almost certainly does not belong in the first iteration. Revisit once the base league has run.

## Open questions

1. **Last-resort tiebreak** if players are still tied at the cut: random draw, or a sudden-death game between the tied players?
2. **Faction selection:** proposing AdSet for all games. Which expansions are owned (the card-based draft needs the Marauder and Homeland setup cards), and which factions are in the curated pool?
3. **Maps and deck:** which map(s), and standard deck or Exiles and Partisans?
4. **Time cap** per game, and the rule when it is hit.
5. **Drop-out policy:** what happens if a player drops before round 4 or between rounds.
6. **Prizes and recognition:** champion, consolation winner.
7. **Schedule:** generate the rounds 1-3 pairings with names and balanced seats.

## Background notes

See [research notes](research-notes.md) for tournament precedents and sources, and [cut analysis](league-variables.md#cut-and-tiebreak-behavior) for simulation context.

- The Root tournaments researched (Root Con 2023, Root Conquest in Orlando, Montreal's annual event, a Wisconsin store event) used bracket formats: single elimination or rounds with winners advancing. No documented Root league with a points table turned up.
- Draft methods seen: AdSet (Root Conquest), plus-one pool with reach (older tournaments), random assignment (a store event), and preference-based tables (Montreal).
- Root Con 2023 stats showed big faction win-rate gaps and seat effects, which is why draft method and seat balance are called out as setup variables.
- Simulation of the 3-round cut (equal-skill players, random winners): clean on wins alone about 36% of the time, about 64% with strength of schedule. A last-resort tiebreak is still needed.
- Simulation of the repeat-faction rule assumed 8 players, random 5-card pools per table, random legal picks, last seat picking first, and a pool of 10-14 factions.
